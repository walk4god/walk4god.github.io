Biblical Diet

The Biblical Diet

Clean and unclean animals from Leviticus chapter 11 (King James Version).

Download the PDF Back to Home

In Leviticus 11, the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron and told Israel which animals they could eat and which they could not. The chapter covers four groups: land animals, water creatures, birds, and insects and creeping things. It ends by giving the reason:

“For I am the LORD your God: ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy.” (Leviticus 11:44)
“To make a difference between the unclean and the clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten.” (Leviticus 11:47)

At a glance

CLEAN: may be eaten
UNCLEAN: may not be eaten
Land animals that have a divided (split) hoof and chew the cud. Both signs are needed. (v. 3)
Land animals missing either sign, such as the camel, rock hyrax, hare and swine (pig) (vv. 4–8), and animals that walk on paws (v. 27).
Water creatures that have both fins and scales. (v. 9)
Water creatures without fins and scales. (vv. 10–12)
Birds that are not on the forbidden list. (The chapter lists only the forbidden birds.)
Birds on the list of 20 in verses 13–19, mostly birds of prey, scavengers and the bat.
Insects: four kinds with jointed legs for leaping: the locust, the destroying locust, the cricket and the grasshopper. (vv. 21–22)
All other flying insects that walk on four legs (vv. 20, 23), and creeping things on the ground, including whatever crawls on its belly (vv. 29–30, 41–42).
PART 1: CLEAN ANIMALS (YOU MAY EAT)

1. Land animals

“Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud, among the beasts, that shall ye eat.” (Leviticus 11:3)

The two-sign test: the animal must (1) have a divided, split hoof and (2) chew the cud, which means it brings food back up to chew again. If either sign is missing, it is unclean.

Leviticus 11 gives the rule but doesn’t name the clean land animals. The parallel chapter, Deuteronomy 14:4–5, names them: “the ox, the sheep, and the goat, the hart, and the roebuck, and the fallow deer, and the wild goat, and the pygarg, and the wild ox, and the chamois.”

2. Water creatures

“These shall ye eat of all that are in the waters: whatsoever hath fins and scales in the waters, in the seas, and in the rivers, them shall ye eat.” (Leviticus 11:9)

The test: the creature must have both fins and scales.

3. Birds

Leviticus 11 doesn’t list clean birds. It lists only the 20 forbidden ones (see Part 2), so birds not on that list may be eaten. Elsewhere the Bible shows Israel eating quail (Exodus 16:13) and offering turtledoves and young pigeons (Leviticus 1:14).

4. Insects

“Yet these may ye eat of every flying creeping thing that goeth upon all four, which have legs above their feet, to leap withal upon the earth; even these of them ye may eat; the locust after his kind, and the bald locust after his kind, and the beetle after his kind, and the grasshopper after his kind.” (Leviticus 11:21–22)

Animals that walk on paws are also unclean: “whatsoever goeth upon his paws, among all manner of beasts that go on all four, those are unclean unto you” (v. 27). Examples: dogs, cats, bears, lions.

2. Water creatures

“And all that have not fins and scales in the seas, and in the rivers … they shall be an abomination unto you.” (Leviticus 11:10)

Anything in the water missing fins or scales is unclean, along with everything that lives in the water that is not a fish (vv. 10–12).

The simple tests to remember
Land: split hoof and chews the cud.   Water: fins and scales.   Birds: not one of the 20 listed.   Insects: only the locust, the destroying locust, the cricket and the grasshopper.   Anything that crawls on its belly: never.

For further study: Deuteronomy 14:3–21 (the same laws repeated), Genesis 7:2 (clean and unclean animals on the ark), Daniel 1:8, Acts 10:9–16 and Mark 7:18–19.

WHAT “DIVIDED HOOF” AND “CHEWS THE CUD” MEAN

These are two things you can check on an animal. It is clean only if it has both.

1. A divided (split) hoof

Look at the animal’s foot. A cow’s hoof is split down the middle into two toes, like two halves. A horse’s hoof is one solid piece, so it isn’t divided. A dog or cat has paws with pads and claws, not hooves at all.

2. Chews the cud

Some animals eat grass quickly and swallow it. Later, while resting, they bring some of it back up from the stomach and chew it again, then swallow it a second time. That re-chewed lump of food is the “cud.” When you see a cow lying in a field with its jaw moving even though it isn’t eating, it is chewing its cud.

How the two work together

AnimalSplit hoof?Chews cud?Result
Cow, sheep, goat, deerYesYesClean
PigYesNoUnclean

The pig is the well-known example. It has the split hoof but doesn’t chew the cud, so it fails, and one sign alone isn’t enough (Leviticus 11:7).

*The rabbit doesn’t bring food back up from its stomach the way a cow does. It re-eats soft droppings to digest its food a second time, and it moves its mouth constantly as if chewing. The Bible’s “chews the cud” for the hare likely describes what people could see.
